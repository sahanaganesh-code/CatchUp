1. **Record** - Zoom meetings or in-person lectures

3. **Get** - Answer + 2-5 timestamped evidence quotes

**Key**: No hallucination - only verifiable information

## Presentation Tips

### Do's ✅
- Lead with the accessibility angle
- Show real empathy for people with disabilities
- Emphasize "health and lifestyle" theme throughout
- Demo the evidence-based Q&A prominently
- Highlight the "no hallucination" trust factor

### Don'ts ❌
- Don't just pitch it as a productivity tool
- Don't minimize the disability experience
- Don't skip showing the evidence quotes
- Don't forget to mention approval gating
- Don't oversell - be honest about MVP status

### Key Messages
1. **Accessibility is a right, not a feature**
2. **Evidence-based = trustworthy for health/education**
3. **User control = respects autonomy**
4. **Real impact on mental health and inclusion**

## Q&A Preparation

**Q: "Why not just use existing tools?"**  
A: "Existing tools transcribe, but don't provide evidence-based Q&A or accessibility-first design. We're purpose-built for people with disabilities."

**Q: "How do you prevent hallucination?"**  
A: "Hard rule: every answer includes 2-5 timestamped quotes from the actual transcript. If evidence doesn't exist, we say 'insufficient evidence' instead of guessing."

**Q: "What's your business model?"**  
A: "Free tier for individuals, paid plans for education/enterprise. Accessibility should be affordable."

**Q: "How is this different from Otter.ai?"**  
A: "Otter transcribes. We provide evidence-based Q&A, smart action proposals, and accessibility-first design. We're solving a different problem."

**Q: "Who are your target users?"**  
A: "Students with disabilities, professionals with ADHD, deaf/hard-of-hearing individuals, anyone who needs meeting accommodations."
